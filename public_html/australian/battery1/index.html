You Already Have Solar Panels. So Why Are You Still Paying High Electricity Bills?

Your panels could be doing more for you. The answer may come down to what happens to the energy your home generates during the day.

You installed solar panels to make better use of the sun and reduce your electricity costs. But if you are still receiving high electricity bills, there may be another factor worth looking at: what happens to the solar energy your home generates during the day?

Many solar households generate more electricity than they use while the sun is shining. Depending on the electricity plan, excess energy may be exported to the grid in exchange for a feed-in tariff. Later, when the sun goes down and household energy use continues, they may need to buy electricity from the grid at a higher rate.

The result? You could be selling excess solar energy during the day and buying electricity back at night.

The problem: selling solar energy cheap and buying it back at a higher rate

During daylight hours, your rooftop solar system generates electricity for your home. When production exceeds what your household needs at that moment, the surplus may be exported to the grid.

The feed-in tariff you receive for that exported electricity may be much lower than the retail rate you pay when importing electricity from the grid. For example:

5cper kWh you are paid for exported solar
25cper kWh you pay to buy electricity back

At those example rates, you receive five times less for each exported unit than you pay for a unit bought back. Your actual rates will depend on your retailer, electricity plan and location.

The solution: store your excess solar energy in a battery

Instead of exporting all your excess daytime solar generation to the grid, a compatible battery can store some of that energy for use later. You can then use the stored electricity in the evening or at night, potentially reducing how much you need to buy from the grid.

How solar battery storage works

  • During the day

    Your panels generate electricity for your home. Excess energy can charge your battery.

  • In the evening

    Your home can use stored solar energy when your panels are producing little or no electricity.

  • At night

    Your battery may help power selected appliances, depending on its capacity, your energy use and its remaining charge.

The result could be greater use of the solar energy you already generate.

Could a solar battery help you reduce your electricity bills?

Adding battery storage may help eligible homeowners make better use of their existing solar systems. Depending on your household's energy usage, battery size, electricity rates, system configuration and applicable incentives, potential savings can vary significantly.

Some offers may advertise savings of up to 80%, but this is not guaranteed and may not apply to every household. Your actual savings will depend on your individual circumstances.

Before buying a battery, it is worth checking your home's suitability and whether any current incentives or installation offers are available.
